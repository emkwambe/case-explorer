# Model card: `credit_pd_xgb` (Solar prime probability of default)

_Generated by `python -m case_analytics.credit.docs --write` from `artifacts/credit-model-snapshot/`. Every number is the snapshot's; `--check` fails if this file and the snapshot disagree._

**Status: developer testing, not independent validation** (the SR 26-2 wording; SR 26-2 replaced SR 11-7 in April 2026). **Second opinion only: the credit policy makes the decision.** The model changes no decision, no locked number and no answer key.

## Intended use

- Training and portfolio demonstration: how a PD model is built, calibrated, explained and checked against a known truth.
- **Not** for real lending decisions, and not for any real applicant. Every borrower, dealer and outcome is synthetic.

> **The model recovers a simple, known simulated process, so its performance does not transfer to real data.** Default here is a logistic monthly hazard of four things: true FICO below 700, true DTI above 36%, a fraud flag, and dealer quality. Real default has many more drivers, noisier data and drift.

## Data

- **Target:** dpd30: 30+ days past due within 18 months (simulated), the Solar book's default flag.
- **Training:** 80,000 funded loans from 8 independent books (seeds 1001, 1002, 1003, 1004, 1005, 1006, 1007, 1008), 2,004 defaults (2.50%).
- **Calibration:** 20,000 funded loans from seeds 1009, 1010, 533 defaults.
- **Test:** the locked seed-42 book, 10,000 funded loans, 225 defaults (2.25%). It played no part in tuning or calibration.
- **Selection bias:** outcomes exist only for funded loans, so the model learns from approved applicants only. Reject inference is Sprint 12.
- **No out-of-time test:** every application in these books is submitted on the same as-of date (2026-01-15); the seed-42 book is out-of-sample by seed only.
- **Two FICO fields.** The credit policy reads the applicant's claimed FICO; the model reads the bureau's (`fico_bureau`). No funded loan claims below the 660 floor (0), but 44 funded loans have a bureau score below 660, all claiming 660–673, which is why the funded book's bureau FICO starts below 660. In this generator the claimed FICO equals the true score on 100.0% of funded loans, while the bureau score is the true score plus noise: bureau minus claimed averages +0.11 points (standard deviation 7.47, range -28 to +28) and is exactly equal on 5.1%; 47 funded loans have a bureau score above 850. The model therefore reads a noisier copy of the FICO that drives the simulated default, which weakens its FICO signal. This is a generator defect, logged in [docs/KNOWN_ISSUES.md](KNOWN_ISSUES.md); the bureau field is kept because it is the one a real lender would use.
- Details, shapes before and after, and the feature list: [analytics/credit/README.md](../analytics/credit/README.md).

**Features** (decision-time only; monotonic direction in brackets): `fico_bureau` [−]; `dti_stated` [+]; `stated_monthly_debts` [+]; `income_stated` [−]; `income_overstatement_pct` [+]; `undisclosed_debt` [+]; `undisclosed_accounts` [+]; `checking_overstatement_pct` [+]; `statement_excess_pct` [+]; `checking_stated` [−]; `checking_statement` [−]; `overdraft_count` [+]; `large_deposits_total` [+]; `tenure_months` [−].

**Excluded:** age, region, zip_code, cost_of_living_index, persona, regime and every audit attribute (sensitive or geographic); the credit bureau's persona field (a generator type, not a lender field); dealer_id (dealer risk belongs to the Dealer View); device and session metadata (fraud signals, not creditworthiness); fraud-rule outputs (reasons come from the raw fields); W-2 wages (a partial-year artefact for short tenures); world truth, the corruption label, dealer quality and PD (answer key only). Hidden truth (the oracle files) is used only to evaluate; a test enforces it.

## Models

- **Baseline `credit_pd_logistic`:** median imputation, standardisation, L2 logistic regression; C = 0.1 chosen by cross-validation over whole training books.
- **Challenger `credit_pd_xgb`:** XGBoost with a monotonic constraint on every feature, no class reweighting (`scale_pos_weight` = 1), histogram trees, one thread, no sampling. Chosen from the grid by cross-validated log loss: max depth 2, learning rate 0.05, 150 trees, min child weight 1.

**Why deciles 1–9 are flat.** The credit policy screens out most of the simulated credit drivers before a loan is funded. In the funded test book the bureau FICO runs 646–867 and the stated back-end DTI 6.1%–36.0%: only 0.19% of funded loans have a true DTI above 36%, where the simulated hazard starts to rise, and 10.8% have a true FICO below 700, where FICO adds risk. ## Policy view (reported only)

At the credit policy's approval count on the seed-42 application pool (10,000 of 18,674), approving the lowest-PD applications instead would swap 1,134 loans in and out under `credit_pd_xgb` and 3,415 under `credit_pd_logistic`. Expected loss (synthetic truth, LGD 0.85): policy $4,413,431, `credit_pd_xgb` $4,445,121, `credit_pd_logistic` $7,064,313. Declined applicants are outside the training population, so their scores are extrapolation. The champion/challenger run through the Policy Sandbox is Sprint 12.

**Why the logistic model's choice costs $7,064,313 against the policy's $4,413,431.** Trained on funded loans only, under a policy that already screens FICO and DTI, the model learns that credit score barely matters: its FICO coefficient is -0.038 per standard deviation. Applied to every applicant, the linear model extrapolates that flat slope to people the policy declined: of its 3,415 swap-ins, 1,466 were policy declines and 5.5% have a FICO below 660; their mean charge-off PD is 3.40% against 1.54% for the loans it swaps out. Monotonic trees flatten beyond the data instead of extrapolating a line, so `credit_pd_xgb` swaps in 1,134 (0.4% below FICO 660) at about the same PD as it swaps out, and costs $4,445,121. Both are scoring applicants outside their training population; Sprint 12's reject inference is the fix for that. The comparison also favours the policy: it reads the claimed FICO, which in this generator equals the true score, while both models read the noisier bureau score. So the models' loss figures are conservative, and part of the logistic model's $7,064,313 is that handicap, not only selection bias.

## Reasons

Method: TreeSHAP splits each prediction's log-odds into one contribution per feature plus a base value; the split was checked to add up on every test loan. Contributions are summed by feature group, and up to the top four groups that raise risk become the reason statements (Reg B, 12 CFR §1002.9, requires the specific principal reasons for adverse action). SHAP is a standard way to rank contributions; it is not a method regulators have endorsed.

**Stability.** Across 20 retrains on resampled training data, a test loan's top reason matched the headline model's 54% of the time on average; 14% of loans kept the same top reason in at least 80% of retrains and 39% in fewer than half. Over all loans the top reason is **unstable**: most loans sit near the average PD, where every contribution is small and the ranking flips easily. In the riskiest tenth of the book, where a reason statement would actually be sent, agreement averages 89% (84% of those loans at 80% or more): stable. (Stability is measured on the top positive contribution, before the materiality threshold below.)

## Reason materiality

A reason group is stated only if its SHAP contribution exceeds **0.005412 log-odds**. The threshold was chosen on the clean funded loans of the calibration books (19,051 loans; the test book plays no part): the smallest value at which at most 5% of clean loans are given a discrepancy reason (on those loans: 41.5% before, 4.4% after). Measured afterwards on the 9,523 clean test loans: 41.8% were given a false discrepancy reason before the threshold and 4.1% after. In the riskiest tenth of the test book (1,000 loans), 0 lose every stated reason (0 had none before). The case view and the case snapshot show stated reasons only.

On the 9,523 clean funded loans the false alarms come from: ASSET_DISCREPANCY 3,979 in the top four (100 as the top reason). All of them are asset discrepancies: stated and statement balances already differ slightly in most clean loans.

**Tampering cannot be separated from clean-loan noise.** Counting every positive contribution, the asset discrepancy was the top reason for 0 of 5 tampering twins and in the top four for 5, but it was also in the top four for 42% of clean funded loans, because stated and statement balances already differ slightly in most clean loans. After the materiality threshold it is stated for 2 of 5 tampering twins (top reason for 0) and for 4.1% of clean funded loans. It is never the top reason, so the model still cannot tell an underwriter that a statement was altered.

**Inflation's perfect score is perfect by construction.** In this simulation an income discrepancy occurs only on fraud loans (0 clean training loans carry one), so any income gap is a fraud signal and the model cannot get it wrong. Real files have benign discrepancies (bonuses, rounding, pay changes), and a real model would have to tell them apart; this score says nothing about that.

The all-applications diagnostic names the asset discrepancy for 5 of 5 fabrication twins (headline, stated: 0 of 5), which confirms that funded-only selection, not the method, is what hides fabrication. It names it for 0 of 5 tampering twins: an open question. The diagnostic does see tampered applications in training, but the generator's tampering also adds large unsourced deposits, which the teaching twins (one altered balance) lack; that is one possible reason, not yet tested.

A low score is a finding, not a failure to hide: it means the model predicts default but cannot tell an underwriter why in fraud terms, which supports keeping the fraud rules beside it. Tampering and fabrication are the hardest case by construction: the funded training books contain neither typology, so the model has only seen the small balance differences of clean loans.

Income band is predicted by stated income, as it must be. The synthetic scenario attributes (family status, disability income) are drawn independently of every financial variable, so they are the control: a proxy there would be a defect. A strong proxy for region or persona means a model could reproduce group differences without ever seeing the attribute, which is why the group tables above are the evidence that matters, not the exclusion of the attribute.

**Legal status, as of October 2026.** ECOA disparate-impact liability was removed from Regulation B effective July 21, 2026, and that change is being litigated (*NFHA v. CFPB*). Disparate impact remains relevant under the Fair Housing Act where it applies, and under state laws such as New Jersey's LAD and Massachusetts law. Colorado SB 26-189 takes effect January 1, 2027. These tables are monitoring evidence for a reviewer; they don't establish or rule out legal compliance.

## Limitations

- Synthetic data from a known, simple process (above); results do not transfer to real lending.
- 225 test defaults: confidence intervals are wide.
- Funded-only selection bias; no reject inference (Sprint 12).
- Fairness evidence is monitoring only (above): sensitive and geographic attributes are excluded and unread, but group outcomes still differ through the credit factors, and none of this is a legal conclusion.
- No out-of-time test (one as-of date). No drift monitoring (Sprint 12).
- Generator defects the model sees (bureau scores above 850; one extra bureau account on most loans) are logged in [docs/KNOWN_ISSUES.md](KNOWN_ISSUES.md) for a future generator version.

## How this would run in production

The feature build would be a dbt model over the loan-origination tables and the retraining a scheduled Airflow job, with the manifest hashes as data contracts. Neither is built here; both are portfolio items for later.

## Reproducibility

`node scripts/export_credit_features.mjs` (data; every file hashed in `data/credit-model/manifest.json`), then `python -m case_analytics.credit.pipeline` (snapshot), with the exact package versions in `analytics/constraints-credit.txt`. A rerun on the same machine is byte-identical; CI rebuilds on Linux and compares numerically within a stated tolerance (`case_analytics.credit.compare`).
