## Available targets

The governed splits carry two distinct label families that can be modelled, and one that cannot.

| Target | Column | Kind of truth | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fraud detection | `label__is_fraud` (with `label__fraud_type`) | Injection ground truth: which typology the generator actually applied | Modelled. Every metric in this card is from this target. |
| Credit default | `label__defaulted` (with `label__seriously_delinquent`, `label__first_payment_default`, `label__months_to_default`, `label__realized_loss`) | Observed outcome ground truth: 36-month simulated loan performance from `src/simulation/performance.js`, computed on world truth for every case including declined ones | Available, not yet modelled by this pipeline. |
| Reference policy opinion | `label__reference_outcome`, `label__reason_codes` | What the generator's own rule engine thought the decision should be | Not a target. Diagnostic only. |

**The shipped model suite predicts injected fraud, not default.** No model in the registry is trained on `label__defaulted`, none of the metrics below says anything about credit risk, and no default model has been fitted, validated or approved. The default target exists in the data and in the split manifests (`bad_rate`, `default_count`, `total_realized_loss`) and is open for future work.

## Intended use

The suite is fraud triage support on **synthetic** mortgage applications, for research and portfolio demonstration. Its purposes are:

- ranking generated cases by fraud likelihood so a threshold policy and its economics can be studied;
- providing a champion and challenger set against which the rule baseline can be compared;
- exercising the governance path end to end: registry lifecycle, validation package, explainability, fairness testing and reason-code mapping.

Model scores never decide anything on their own. In this system the underwriting policy in `src/decisioning/underwriting/policy.js` produces the outcome and its governed reason codes; the model contributes a triage signal and its explanation.

### Out of scope

The following uses are explicitly out of scope and are not supported by any evidence in this repository:

- any real lending decision, pricing, pre-screening or account action;
- adverse action against a real person, or any input to an adverse-action notice;
- any production credit or fraud decisioning system;
- credit-risk modelling, loss forecasting or capital work. The dataset now carries a simulated default outcome, but it is generated from assumed hazard and loss parameters and no model here is fitted to it.

The model has never been evaluated on real data and no result here transfers to a real applicant population.

## Metrics and why they were chosen

| Metric | Why |
|---|---|
| PR-AUC | Fraud is the minority class in every split (test fraud rate 0.1989). Accuracy and ROC-AUC are dominated by the negative class; precision-recall area reflects performance where the decision actually lives. It is the primary champion-selection metric. |
| Calibration error (ECE) and Brier | Scores drive a threshold policy and the decision-economics simulator, so they must be usable as probabilities rather than only as a ranking. The comparison narrative treats ECE under 0.05 as the bar for using scores without post-hoc recalibration. |
| False-positive rate | Every false positive consumes review capacity and imposes customer friction. FPR is reported on every split and on the clean negative control, where any alert is by construction a false positive. |
| Recall, precision, F1, FNR, specificity | Reported alongside for completeness at the selected threshold. |
| Per-fraud-type and per-regime slices | A pooled score can hide a typology the model never catches. |

## Training data

The champion is trained on the `train` split only: 3,598 rows, 677 with a fraud typology (0.1882). Rows contain the 55 permitted decision features, which are claims, evidence values, claim-versus-evidence discrepancies, underwriting ratios and application metadata. Prohibited columns (identity, world truth, labels, and any discrepancy computed against truth) are rejected at runtime by `enforceFeaturePolicy`; sensitive columns are held out of training entirely. Threshold selection uses `validation`; `test` and the four stress splits are untouched during fitting.

The comparison narrative attributes the champion gain over the rules baseline (+0.019 PR-AUC) to continuous ranking: the rules emit a small set of discrete scores and cannot order cases within a severity tier. Note that `gradient_boosting` has materially better calibration (ECE 0.002 versus 0.034).

## Fairness evaluation

Recorded in `analytics/artifacts/fairness-report.json` (model layer) and `artifacts/validation-summary.json` (generator and decision layers).

**Counterfactual invariance (model layer).** 300 rows tested, 22,800 counterfactual pairs formed by flipping sensitive attributes and rescoring. Changed outcomes: 0. Invariance rate 1.000. Status `PASS`.

**Counterfactual invariance (decision layer).** 300 cases tested, 4,311 pairs, 0 changed, invariance rate 1.000, and the `counterfactual_invariance` release gate passes.

**Subgroup analysis.** The generator-level fairness module reports a minimum selection ratio of 1.000 on the independently sampled scenario attributes (`family_status_scenario`, `disability_income_scenario`) with no significant disparities, so the `scenario_attribute_parity` gate passes. It nevertheless returns overall status `WARN` because derived attributes fall below four-fifths parity: income band under 50K at 0.112, income band 50K to 80K at 0.409, `RETAIL_MANAGER` at 0.483, `TEACHER` at 0.592, `CONSTRUCTION_WORKER` at 0.599, age band 22 to 29 at 0.599, `ENTREPRENEUR` at 0.654, income band 80K to 120K at 0.624, age band 50 to 59 at 0.693, age band 30 to 39 at 0.733 and age band 40 to 49 at 0.766. These attributes are deterministic functions of the generated financial life and are correlated with outcomes by construction, so the disparity is expected and is reported rather than suppressed.

**This is a technical control, not a legal compliance determination.** The artifacts carry their own disclaimer: technical fairness-testing controls only, results do not establish legal compliance, they support human legal and compliance review. No race, ethnicity, sex, national origin or religion is modelled anywhere in the generator, and the attributes tested are synthetic scenario labels. Passing these checks on synthetic data says nothing about disparate impact on real applicants under ECOA, the Fair Housing Act, Regulation B or any other regime.

## Caveats and recommendations

1. **The task is near-separable on synthetic data.** Every model in the suite scores above 0.97 test PR-AUC, including the deterministic rules baseline at 0.976. The champion ranking rests on a 0.006 margin over logistic regression and 0.002 over gradient boosting. Do not read the ranking as evidence that random forests are better for fraud detection; it is evidence that this generated task is easy and that the four strategies agree.
2. **The fraud label is injection truth, and the rule baseline was written against the same operators.** `label__is_fraud` and `label__fraud_type` are not policy-derived: they record which typology the generator applied, so training on them is not circular with the labelling policy. The circularity that does remain is between the injected operators and the deterministic rule baseline, which was written to target those same operators with matching thresholds, so precision and recall of the rules report agreement between two descriptions of one injection mechanism. The policy-derived column, `label__reference_outcome`, is a diagnostic and is not a training target for any model here. Section 6 of `docs/methodology/LIMITATIONS.md` states this in full.
3. **The default outcome exists but is simulated from assumed parameters.** Every row now carries 36-month performance from `src/simulation/performance.js`, which reads world truth and never reads the policy, so grading a decision against it is not circular. It is still not a real outcome: the job-loss hazard, expense shock, income volatility and loss-given-default terms are hand-chosen assumptions, and two typologies default by an asserted intent probability rather than by mechanism. Absolute default and loss levels from this data are not credible; only the relative ordering is. Sections 7 to 10 of `docs/methodology/LIMITATIONS.md` set this out. Separately, the 470,820 USD expected-loss figure quoted above is not from that simulation at all: it is a loan amount multiplied by an assumed loss-given-fraud rate of 0.35, and remains arithmetic on assumptions.
4. **Parameters are not fitted to any real data.** No parameter in `src/distribution_config.json` reaches the `EMPIRICALLY_CALIBRATED` provenance class; see `docs/methodology/PARAMETER_SOURCES.md`.
5. **Small typology cells.** Seven of nine typologies have fewer than 50 test cases and four have fewer than 20. Treat per-typology recall as directional.
6. **Calibration is adequate but not the best available.** The champion sits at ECE 0.034 against the stated 0.05 bar, while the gradient-boosting challenger reaches 0.002. If scores are used as probabilities rather than as a ranking, prefer the better-calibrated model or recalibrate the champion.
7. **Recommended practice.** Keep champion and challenger both live, re-run the pipeline on any change to the generator, ruleset, policy or reason codes, and compare manifests rather than raw numbers: a metric is only comparable across runs that share the dataset version, configuration hash and generator commit.
